Neighborhood Playdate · last updated 26 September 2026

The short version

Neighborhood Playdate helps a parent arrange a first playdate with a family nearby, inside a week, without a group chat. You post when you're free, see which families on your block overlap, and send one invitation with a time and a public place. Once you both say yes, the rest happens in Messages.

Questions, problems, or a family to report: julien.lerosty@gmail.com. A person reads every message.

Joining

Playdate is invite-only and opens one block at a time. The first screen says so and offers three ways in.

  • "I have an invite code". For a code from a neighbor who is already on. They make one under Settings → Invite a neighbor. A neighbor's code works once, so each new family needs its own.
  • "I don't have a code yet". You sign in, tell the app roughly where you live, and join the waiting list. We'll let you know when a block opens near you.
  • "Already on Playdate? Sign in". For a new phone, or after signing out. Sign in with the same phone number and your family is there as before. No code needed.

You sign in with your phone number. We text you a six-digit code; there is no password. If it doesn't arrive, check the number and tap "Send a new code", which is ready after 30 seconds. If the app says too many codes were sent, wait a few minutes and try again.

Where you live. Use your current location, or type your cross-streets. Either way, Playdate receives only a point rounded on your phone to a 200-meter square. Typed cross-streets are looked up by Apple's geocoding service first; the privacy policy has the details. If the app can't find a corner, check the spelling or try a nearby street.

If you see "Not on this block yet", either your area hasn't opened or you joined without a code. Either way, you are on the waiting list. We keep a rough, 200-meter version of your location so we can tell you when a block opens near you, and nothing else. If a neighbor gives you a code later, tap "I have an invite code" on that screen. It also lets you sign out or delete your account.

If the app asks you to update, install the latest version from the App Store. It asks only when your version can no longer work with the service.

Using it

  • Post a window. The hours you're actually free, and what you'd like to do. Windows vanish once they pass.
  • See who overlaps. Families on your block whose free hours cross yours, closest first.
  • Propose. One time, one public place. They have 24 hours to answer.
  • Say hello. After you both accept, you each get the other's phone number once, and Messages takes over.
  • Afterwards, one question: did it happen? It is the only thing the app keeps score of.

Settings is the person icon at the top left of the Windows tab. From there you can invite a neighbor, suggest a spot, change your kids' ages, manage blocked families, and find the privacy policy, the terms of use and how to reach us.

Suggest a spot when the map is missing a park or a library. We look at each suggestion before it appears on the map.

Language

Playdate is in English and Simplified Chinese, and follows your iPhone's language. To choose a language for Playdate alone, open your iPhone's Settings app, find Playdate, and pick one. That choice appears only when your iPhone has more than one preferred language; add one under Settings → General → Language & Region.

Notifications

Playdate sends three kinds: a new proposal, a yes, and the evening question about whether a playdate happened. They arrive in the language Playdate was last opened in. If they stop arriving, check Settings → Notifications → Playdate on your iPhone.

Safety

  • First playdates are always at a public spot. Homes unlock only after you've met once.
  • Children are an age range and, if you choose, a gender. Never a name, a birthday, or a photo.
  • Your exact location never leaves your phone. It is rounded to a 200-meter square before anything is sent, and neighbors see only a distance, rounded to about 400 meters.
  • Block or report from the menu on any match or proposal. Blocking is quiet and mutual: neither family sees the other again, and nobody is told. A report reaches us straight away, and we act on every report within 24 hours. Manage blocks under Settings → Blocked families.
  • Some words aren't allowed. Names and suggested spots are checked against a list of objectionable words. If you see "That wording isn't allowed here", try something else.
  • Everyone agrees to the terms of use: parents and guardians aged 18 or over only, and zero tolerance for objectionable content or abusive behavior.

Deleting your account

Settings → Delete my account removes your family, your photo, your children's age ranges, your windows, your playdates and your sign-in, at once and permanently. There is no separate request to make. If you haven't joined a block yet, the same button is on the "Not on this block yet" screen.
