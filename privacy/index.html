Privacy policy

Neighborhood Playdate · last updated 26 September 2026

The short version

Neighborhood Playdate helps a parent arrange a first playdate with a family nearby. It does that with as little information as the job allows.

  • Children are an age range and, optionally, a gender. Never a name, a birthday, or a photo.
  • Your exact location never leaves your phone. It is rounded to a roughly 200-meter square on the device before anything is sent, and neighbors see only a distance, rounded to about 400 meters. If you type your cross-streets or address instead, Apple's geocoding service looks them up, and the point it returns is rounded the same way on your phone.
  • A street address is kept only if you type one. It is shown once, and only to a family that has agreed to a playdate at your home.
  • Your phone number is shared once, with one family, after you have both agreed to a playdate.
  • There is no advertising, no analytics, and no tracking, and nothing is sold or shared with data brokers.

Using the app also means agreeing to the terms of use.

About your children

An age range, and a gender if you choose to give one. That is the entire list.

No names, no birthdays, no photographs, no school, no anything else — there is nowhere in the app to type them and nowhere in the database to keep them. Leaving the gender unset is a permanent, equal answer; the app never asks twice and never marks it as missing.

Neighbors on your block see the age ranges and any genders you set. That is what matching is.

Before you join a block: the waiting list

You can sign in without an invite code and join the waiting list. The app then keeps your sign-in (your phone number) and a rough location — the same roughly 200-meter square, rounded on your phone — tied to that sign-in. That is so the app can show you the waiting list when you come back, and so you can be told when a block opens near you. The same happens if the home you enter is outside every block that has opened. Nobody else sees it.

About location, specifically

This is the part worth reading twice.

When you use your device's location, the app rounds it on your phone, straight away, to a grid roughly 200 meters across. The precise position is never stored, never written to a log, and never uploaded. So you can check what was found, the app then asks Apple's geocoding service for the street names at the rounded point.

When you type your cross-streets or an address instead, the phone cannot turn them into coordinates on its own. The app sends the street names and city you typed to Apple's geocoding service, which returns a point. That point is rounded to the same 200-meter grid on your phone before anything reaches Playdate.

Either way, Playdate's servers receive only the rounded point, never your precise device location.

What a neighbor sees is a distance — "0.5 miles" — computed from those rounded points and then rounded again, to the nearest 400 meters (about a quarter of a mile), and never shown as less than that. Exact distances between squares, compared across a few accounts, could narrow down which square a family lives in; the second rounding stops that.

Your street address is a separate thing. It exists only if you type one in Settings, it is shared only when you have both agreed to a playdate at your home, and it is shown once at that moment rather than saved to their phone.

What is never collected

No advertising identifiers. No analytics or crash-reporting services. No tracking across apps or websites — the app contains no third-party tracking code of any kind. No contacts, no photo library, no calendar, no browsing history, no precise or background location. No message content: once two families agree, the conversation happens in your own Messages app, which this app cannot see.

Who your information is shared with

Other families on your block see what is listed in the tables above, and nothing else.

Service providers who process data so the app can work:

  • Supabase — the database, sign-in, file storage and server functions.
  • Twilio — sends the SMS code you sign in with, on Supabase's behalf.
  • Apple — delivers push notifications, if you allow them.
  • Apple's geocoding service — turns the cross-streets or address you type into a point on the map, and a rounded point back into street names for you to check.

Each of these handles data only to provide the service. Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared for advertising. There are no data brokers.

The operator is notified when someone reports a family, including the reason given, so the report can be acted on. The operator also reviews each public spot a family suggests before it appears on the map.

Law enforcement only where a valid legal requirement applies.

Children

This app is for parents and guardians aged 18 or over. Children do not have accounts, do not sign in, and do not use it. Nothing is knowingly collected from a child.

The only information about a child is the age range and optional gender a parent enters. Because that is all the app holds, there is no children's contact information, location, photograph, or identifier to protect — the design is the protection.

If you believe a child has somehow provided information, write to julien.lerosty@gmail.com and it will be deleted.

How long it is kept, and how to delete it

Your free-time windows disappear on their own once they have passed.

Everything else is kept while your account exists. Settings → Delete my account removes, at once and permanently: your family, your photo, your children's age ranges and genders, your windows, your device tokens, your private notes, the families you blocked or reported, the invite codes you created, every proposal involving you, and your sign-in itself. A public spot you suggested stays for your neighbors, with nothing connecting it to you.

If you haven't joined a block yet, the same Delete my account button is on the waiting-list screen. It removes your place on the waiting list and your sign-in.

There is no recovery after deletion, and no separate request to make — the button does it. If you can't reach it, write to julien.lerosty@gmail.com and it will be done for you.

Your rights

You can see everything the app holds about you inside the app, change your name, photo, address, usual place, and your children's ages and genders at any time, and delete all of it with the button above. Depending on where you live you may also have rights to a copy of your data or to object to how it is handled; write to julien.lerosty@gmail.com and it will be honored.

Security

Everything travels encrypted. On the server, each family can read only its own rows, enforced by the database itself rather than by application code. Phone numbers and addresses are returned only at the moment an agreed playdate needs them, by a function that checks both families agreed.

No system is perfect, and this one is run by a small operator. If you find a weakness, please write to julien.lerosty@gmail.com rather than publishing it.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects you, the date at the top changes and you will be told in the app before the change applies.

Neighborhood Playdate is invite-only and works one block at a time.
